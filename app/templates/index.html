Okay, so let's get started. Today we're talking about photosynthesis, which is, honestly, the reason almost everything on this planet is alive. So at the simplest level, photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria take light energy and store it as chemical energy, in sugar. The overall equation, which you've probably seen before, is six carbon dioxide plus six water, with light, gives you one glucose and six oxygen. Now, where does this happen? In the chloroplast. Inside the chloroplast you've got these stacks of membranes called thylakoids, and around them a fluid called the stroma. Keep those two words in mind, because the two stages of photosynthesis happen in those two places. The first stage is the light-dependent reactions. These happen in the thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll absorbs light, mostly red and blue, which is why leaves look green to us, they reflect the green. That energy splits water molecules, and that's actually where the oxygen we breathe comes from, it comes from the water, not the carbon dioxide, people get that wrong on the exam every year. The energy is captured in two molecules, ATP and NADPH. Then the second stage, the Calvin cycle, happens in the stroma. It doesn't need light directly, but it does need the ATP and NADPH from stage one. An enzyme called RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto a five-carbon sugar, and after a series of steps you get G3P, which the plant uses to build glucose and other sugars. So, to sum up: light reactions make energy carriers and release oxygen, the Calvin cycle spends those carriers to turn carbon dioxide into sugar. Next week we'll look at what limits the rate of this whole process.

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria store light energy as chemical energy in sugar. It happens in the chloroplast in two stages. The light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membranes use light absorbed by chlorophyll to split water, releasing oxygen and producing ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle in the stroma uses that ATP and NADPH, with the enzyme RuBisCO, to fix carbon dioxide into G3P, which is built into glucose.

Questions

Which video formats can I upload?

MP4, MOV, MKV, WEBM, AVI and M4V, up to 2 GB per file. Any recording with a clear audio track works, including phone videos and screen recordings.

How long does processing take?

It depends on the length of the lecture and the machine running the app. On a laptop CPU, expect it to take about as long as the video itself; with a GPU it is several times faster. The first run also downloads the models.

Is my lecture sent to a third-party service?

No. Whisper and BART run on the server that hosts this app. Your video stays in its uploads folder until you delete the lecture.

Does it work for lectures that aren't in English?

Whisper transcribes dozens of languages. The BART summarizer was trained on English news, so summaries are most reliable for English lectures.

What happens with very long lectures?

The transcript is split into parts of about 600 words, each part is summarized, and the partial summaries are condensed into one.
